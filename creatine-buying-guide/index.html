How to choose a creatine

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Creatine monohydrate is the most researched, safest, and most affordable form. Stick with 3–5 grams daily (no loading needed for most people) from a reputable brand that tests for purity and dissolves cleanly in water. Powder is cheaper than capsules; capsules offer convenience if you don’t mind the premium.

What form of creatine should I buy?

Creatine monohydrate is the gold standard. Every other form (ethyl ester, hydrochloride, buffered) claims to improve absorption or reduce side effects, but independent research consistently shows monohydrate delivers the same results at a fraction of the price. You are paying mostly for marketing with the fancy forms. Within monohydrate, you have two main choices: powder or capsules. Powder is more economical per serving and mixes easily into water or a drink. Capsules are convenient for travel or for those who dislike the texture of powder, but you often need to swallow several capsules to get the standard daily dose (3–5 grams). For most buyers, a simple unflavored micronized monohydrate powder is the best value. Micronized means the particles are ground finer, so they dissolve more completely and cause less bloating.

How do I judge quality and purity?

Look for brands that third-party test their creatine for purity and heavy metals. Reputable companies will display a certificate of analysis or mention testing by organizations like USP, NSF, or Informed Sport. If a label says “cGMP certified” or “pharmaceutical grade,” that is a good start, but independent verification is stronger. You can do a simple home test: mix a scoop of creatine in warm water. Pure creatine monohydrate will dissolve partially and settle at the bottom; if it stays completely suspended or forms a gel, that suggests fillers or other ingredients. Also, pure creatine has a faint, slightly bitter taste. An overly sweet or flavored product may mask poor quality. Avoid proprietary blends that hide the exact amount of creatine. You want a single ingredient: 100% creatine monohydrate.

Do I need to load creatine or cycle it?

Loading (taking 20 grams per day for 5–7 days) saturates your muscles faster, but it is not necessary. A daily dose of 3–5 grams will achieve full saturation within three to four weeks with less risk of digestive upset. If you want fast results for an upcoming event, a short loading phase works; otherwise, skip it. Cycling (stopping every few months) is also unnecessary. Unlike many supplements, creatine does not downregulate your body’s natural production permanently, and studies show long-term use of 3–5 grams daily is safe. There is no known benefit to taking breaks. The only exception: people with pre-existing kidney conditions should consult a doctor before any creatine use, but that is rare.

What about bloating and stomach issues?

Creatine draws water into your muscle cells, not into your belly. The “bloating” most people experience is actually water retention inside muscles, which can make you look fuller, not puffy. True stomach discomfort usually comes from poor mixing or taking too much at once. To minimize any chance of cramping or gas, start with a small dose (2–3 grams) for a few days, then increase. Mix your powder in at least 8 ounces of water and drink it slowly. If you are sensitive, avoid consuming it with a lot of caffeine or acidic juice, some people report less irritation when they take it with just water. If you still have problems, try a micronized powder or switch to a capsule form. Capsules bypass the taste issue and are easier on some stomachs, though you pay more.

How should I take creatine and when?

Consistency matters more than timing. Take 3–5 grams at roughly the same time each day. Many people prefer post-workout because it often gets mixed into a protein shake, but taking it pre-workout is equally effective. The difference is negligible. Creatine is not a stimulant, so it does not matter if you take it on an empty stomach or with food. However, pairing it with a carbohydrate source (like juice or a sports drink) may improve absorption slightly, thanks to the insulin spike, but water works fine for most. A common mistake is assuming more is better. Sticking to the standard daily dose avoids digestive stress and waste. There is no evidence that mega-dosing beyond 10 grams confers extra benefits after saturation.

What should a good label tell me?

A trustworthy label lists “creatine monohydrate” as the only ingredient. Avoid anything that says “patented” or “proprietary blend” without disclosing exact amounts. Servings should be clearly stated, usually 5 grams per scoop. Check the suggested use. If it advises taking 10 grams or more daily for maintenance, that is overkill. Reputable brands recommend 3–5 grams. Also look for a “purity” or “assay” statement on the label or website. Some brands list “99.8% or higher purity” right on the bottle. Finally, expiration dates matter. Creatine degrades into creatinine over time, especially if exposed to moisture or heat. A fresh container from a high-turnover seller is best. If the powder clumps or smells like ammonia, it has gone bad.

Frequently asked questions

Is creatine safe for long-term use?

Yes. Hundreds of studies show that 3–5 grams daily for years poses no health risks for healthy adults. The only caution is for people with pre-existing kidney disease; they should consult a doctor.

Does creatine make you gain weight?

Creatine increases water content inside muscle cells, which adds a few pounds of water weight initially. This is normal and not fat gain. It also helps you lift heavier, which can lead to lean muscle mass over time.

Can I take creatine with coffee or pre-workout?

Yes. Caffeine does not negate creatine’s benefits despite old myths. Some people report mild stomach discomfort from the combination, but that is individual. If it bothers you, space them out by an hour.

Do women need a different type of creatine?

No. The same creatine monohydrate works equally well for women. Some brands market pink or special blends, but these are unnecessary gimmicks. Dosage and protocol are identical.

How long until I see results?

With loading (20g/day), you may feel a difference in strength or recovery within a week. With 3–5g daily, noticeable improvements typically appear after 2–4 weeks. Creatine is not a stimulant; it helps you train harder over time.

Should I buy flavored creatine?

Flavored creatine can be convenient, but it usually contains artificial sweeteners, fillers, and adds cost. Unflavored micronized creatine mixes tastelessly into any beverage and is generally purer and cheaper.

In shortFocus on a pure, third-party tested creatine monohydrate powder from a reputable brand, take 3–5 grams daily, and forget the rest. The fancy forms and complex protocols are just noise. If you want an easy, effective, and affordable boost to strength and recovery, this simple approach is backed by decades of evidence.
